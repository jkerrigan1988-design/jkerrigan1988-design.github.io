Example walkthrough

See it work, before it touches anything real.

This page narrates the Pacifica Sweep review workflow using synthetic example data. Nothing here connects to your files, and none of the example actions are real.

Example — synthetic data. The folder, files, and recommendations below are invented for illustration. The real product follows the same rule throughout: it recommends, you decide.

How the example flows

1 You choose what it sees

Example: you point Pacifica Sweep at a folder called Photos. Nothing is opened, read, or listed until you make that choice — connecting a source is always your explicit decision, and you can disconnect it anytime.

2 It inventories and recommends

Example findings from the synthetic folder. Duplicate detection compares exact file contents, so only byte-identical files are grouped — nothing is guessed:

  • beach-IMG_2041.jpg and beach-IMG_2041-copy.jpg — identical contents. Recommendation: archive one copy. You pick which.
  • old-installer.dmg — listed for your review with only a low-confidence keep suggestion. Your call.
  • Taxes-2024.pdf — you marked this Keep earlier, so it is protected: any future suggestion for it stays pinned to Keep, and only your explicit action can change it. Protected stays protected.
3 You review — keep, archive, or tidy

Example: in your review queue, each suggestion waits for you. You keep the sharper beach photo, archive the duplicate, and leave the installer alone. Marking something Keep protects it from being suggested again. Nothing expires and nothing is auto-approved while you think it over.

4 You approve — and only then

Example: you approve archiving one duplicate. Only that approved action runs — whether it runs now or on a schedule you set. Pacifica Sweep never deletes, moves, or reorganizes anything without your explicit approval, and nothing runs in the background on your device.
