Trust & Privacy

Your control, in writing. Here's exactly what Pacifica Sweep can see, what it does with it, and what it will never do.

Our commitments

What we promise

  • You approve every action. Nothing is deleted, moved, archived, or reorganized without your explicit approval — no exceptions, no "smart" overrides.
  • Local-first. The app runs on your machine and works with the files and sources on it. Reviewing happens locally.
  • Nothing runs on your device while closed. No local background workers — when the app is closed on your machine, it's off. If you connect to the Pacifica Sweep platform and create scheduled maintenance tasks, those run on the server on the schedule you set (never on your device). Each run refreshes your inventory and drafts recommendations — deleting, moving, or archiving still waits for your explicit approval — and you can see, pause, or delete the tasks in Tasks.
  • Explicit connections only. Pacifica Sweep sees only the folders, libraries, and accounts you deliberately connect. Disconnect anytime.
  • Full audit trail. Every approval and action is recorded in your decision history, reviewable at any time.
  • No surprises in the fine print. If a feature needs a permission, it asks first and explains why.

Honest limitations

What Pacifica Sweep is not

We'd rather tell you plainly than let you assume:

  • It is not an automatic cleaner — there is no mode where it acts without your approval.
  • It cannot access accounts, devices, or folders you haven't connected.
  • Pacifica Sweep does not monitor you in the background or while the app is closed on your device. Server-side scheduled tasks you create run only on the schedule you set, and only for the workspaces you chose.
  • It does not sell data or show advertising. The business model under consideration is a straightforward subscription — pricing isn't finalized.
  • It won't fix what it can't see: unconnected drives, other people's devices, and paper piles are out of scope.

Security, stated carefully: Pacifica Sweep is built local-first with explicit approvals and an audit trail, which keeps your data under your control. We don't make sweeping guarantees about being "unhackable" — no honest company can. What we can say is that the design minimizes exposure: your file contents are never uploaded to our servers — the only exception is content you explicitly hand the app yourself, like a JSON inventory import. When you connect a service like Dropbox, only file metadata (names, paths, sizes) is stored so the app can inventory it — contents stay where they are — and nothing acts without you. The one exception is encrypted offsite backups, which you can enable to store encrypted snapshots in an S3-compatible bucket you configure: they are encrypted with your key before they leave, and only your key can read them back.
